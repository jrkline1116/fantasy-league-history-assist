What Belongs in a Fantasy Football League Record Book

Updated October 13, 2026

A record book turns a fantasy league from a yearly contest into a running story. The best leagues have one, and the arguments it settles (and starts) are half the fun. Here's what's worth tracking and how to keep it honest over the years.

Single-game records

  • Highest score. The headline record. Everyone remembers where they were.
  • Lowest score. The record nobody wants. Usually involves a bye week someone forgot.
  • Biggest blowout. Largest margin of victory.
  • Closest game. Smallest margin, often decided by a kicker on Monday night.
  • Highest combined score. The best game two teams played together.
  • Highest score in a loss and lowest score in a win. The purest luck records there are.

Season records

  • Most points per game in a season, and the fewest.
  • Best and worst regular-season record.
  • Luckiest and unluckiest season, by the gap between record and all-play record.
  • Champion with the worst regular season, the ultimate "get in and get hot."

All-time records

  • Most titles and most finals appearances.
  • Best average finish among managers with a real track record (at least half the league's seasons is a sensible bar).
  • Longest winning and losing streaks, counted across seasons.
  • Longest title drought among founding members.
  • Most lopsided rivalry: the head-to-head matchup one manager owns.

Keeping records fair as the league changes

Few leagues use the same rules for ten years. A few habits keep old records comparable:

  • Use per-game numbers for seasons. The NFL went from 16 to 17 games in 2021 and many leagues added a regular-season week. Points per game compares fairly; season totals don't.
  • Note scoring changes. A switch from standard to PPR scoring adds a point for every catch, which can mean 20 or more extra points a week for a team. A single-game record set under PPR isn't directly comparable to one from standard. Write the year of the change down somewhere everyone can see.
  • Decide what counts as a game. Do playoff games count toward streaks? Consolation games? Median-score wins? Pick a rule and keep it. This site counts regular-season games for records and streaks, adds winners-bracket games to head-to-head, and leaves consolation games out.
  • Merge returning managers. When someone comes back on a new account, combine the accounts so their records follow them.

Making it part of the league

  • Read the new records at the draft. A new all-time low makes a great opening toast.
  • Give the record holder something: a line in the league name, a mention in the group chat, a trophy plate.
  • Keep the record book where everyone can open it on a phone during the season, not in a commissioner's spreadsheet.
